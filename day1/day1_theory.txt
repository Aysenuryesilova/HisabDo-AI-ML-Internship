# Day 1 - AI/ML Theoretical Foundations

## Core Concepts & Definitions

### 1. Artificial Intelligence (AI)

- **Definition:** A broad field of computer science dedicated to creating systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as reasoning, decision-making, and problem-solving.
- **How it works:** It encompasses both rule-based systems (if/else logic) and data-driven learning systems.
- **Real-World Example:** Game-playing AI like Stockfish (chess engine) or automated route planning in Google Maps.

### 2. Machine Learning (ML)

- **Definition:** A subset of AI that enables systems to learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.
- **How it works:** Instead of hardcoding rules, ML models rely on statistical techniques to find patterns in training data and make predictions on new data.
- **Real-World Example:** Email Spam Filters (detecting spam based on word patterns and user flag history).

### 3. Deep Learning (DL)

- **Definition:** A specialized subfield of Machine Learning based on Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) with multiple layers (hence "deep").
- **How it works:** It automatically extracts features from large amounts of unstructured data (images, audio, text) without human feature engineering.
- **Real-World Example:** Autonomous Vehicles (Tesla Autopilot analyzing real-time video streams to identify pedestrians and lanes).

### 4. Generative AI (GenAI)

- **Definition:** A branch of Deep Learning designed to generate original content—such as text, images, audio, or code—based on learned data patterns.
- **How it works:** Powered by modern architectures like Transformers and Diffusion Models, it predicts and crafts the most probable next elements to create novel outputs.
- **Real-World Example:** Large Language Models like ChatGPT for text synthesis or Midjourney for AI image generation.
